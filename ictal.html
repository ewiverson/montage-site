About

PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering

With more than a decade of research and development in the semiconductor industry

Translational research experience

Taking research concepts to volume production through experimental design, electrical testing, data analysis, and computational modeling

A decade of measuring small things

I spent more than ten years measuring tiny currents and voltages in semiconductor devices, fitting models to noisy data, and taking results from a bench setup to volume production. The same problems show up in neural recordings, mostly noise and calibration.

A personal stake

My youngest daughter was born in 2023 with focal cortical dysplasia and has undergone four epilepsy surgeries, including a hemispherotomy. I’ve dedicated the remainder of my career to doing everything I can to help children with drug-resistant epilepsy.

Services

Low-noise measurement

Grounding, shielding, noise floor, and amplifier and electrode choices for recording setups. I can troubleshoot an existing rig or scope a new one.

Time-series analysis

Cleaning up long EEG and SEEG recordings and pulling out the features your study needs.

Statistical and simulation modeling

Fitting models to measured data, power analysis, and Monte Carlo methods.

Data standardization

Converting recordings to BIDS or NWB so they’re ready to share.

Research code

Turning lab scripts into tested, documented Python, with version control and reproducible environments so others can run it.

De-identification tools

Built and tested on synthetic data, so I never handle patient records.

Planning a grant application?

If your aims involve signal analysis, measurement, or data sharing, let’s talk for 30 minutes, free. Bring a rough aim, a description of your data, or a problem you’re stuck on. I’ll tell you honestly whether I can help and how I’d approach it.

If it makes sense, I can help you scope my part of the work: what it involves, how many days it takes, and what you’d get. I’ll write the methods and budget language for those pieces and send you a biosketch and a short letter for the application.

How I work

Before I start, we agree on what you’re getting: the deliverable, the file format, and how we’ll check that it works. Small projects can be a fixed price. Open-ended work is hourly or by the day.

The code is yours. I document it well enough that someone else in your lab can pick it up.

Talking with epilepsy teams

If you work at a company building devices, diagnostics, or treatments for epilepsy, I’m happy to meet with your team, by video or in person if we’re close, and tell you what drug-resistant epilepsy looks like from the family side.

I can talk about how we learned what our options were, what the decisions felt like, what daily life with monitoring and devices is like, and what I wish the people building these tools understood. Sessions are usually 30 to 60 minutes. A first conversation is free.

I’m a parent and an engineer, not a clinician, so I speak only about our own experience and not about medical advice.
