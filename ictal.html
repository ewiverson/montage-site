About

Hi, I’m Eric Iverson, an electrical engineer whose life was changed forever by his daughter’s drug-resistant epilepsy. Now I dedicate my work to helping engineers and researchers who are working to build a better future for families like mine.

PhD in Electrical and Computer Engineering

With more than a decade of research and development in the semiconductor industry

Translational research experience

Taking research concepts to volume production through experimental design, electrical testing, data analysis, and computational modeling

Electrical characterization and noise analysis

Extensive experience in low-current, low-voltage, and low-noise electrical measurement, SPICE and circuit modeling, and statistical model calibration using Monte Carlo simulation. These methods carry over to biopotential acquisition and to the analysis of intracranial EEG and other electrophysiological signals.

A personal stake

My youngest daughter was born in 2023 with focal cortical dysplasia and has undergone four epilepsy surgeries, including a hemispherotomy. I’ve dedicated the remainder of my career to doing everything I can to help children with drug-resistant epilepsy.

Services

Low-noise measurement system design for biopotential acquisition

Scoping of signal-chain architecture, electrode and amplifier selection, input-referred noise budgeting, and calibration strategy, so that noise floor and dynamic range targets are validated before hardware is committed.

Time-series analysis of EEG, iEEG/SEEG, and other physiological signals

Preprocessing, artifact rejection, and quality control; spectral and connectivity feature extraction; and event detection, for example of interictal epileptiform discharges and seizures.

Statistical and simulation modeling

Statistical model calibration, Monte Carlo simulation, power analysis, and parameter estimation from measured data.

Data standardization and sharing preparation

BIDS (including iEEG-BIDS) and NWB conversion, metadata annotation, validation, and preparation of archive-ready datasets in support of NIH data management and sharing requirements.

Research software and reproducibility

Refactoring of laboratory analysis code into tested, documented Python packages, with version control, automated testing, and containerized environments.

De-identification and database tooling

Development and validation of de-identification and database tools using synthetic data, so that no protected health information is accessed.

Contributing to grant applications

For applications with aims involving signal analysis, measurement system design, or data sharing, I offer a complimentary 30-minute scoping call. A rough aim, a description of the data, or a specific technical question is sufficient preparation. I will assess whether I can contribute and outline a proposed technical approach.

Where there is a fit, I can define the scope of my contribution (tasks, level of effort, and deliverables), draft the corresponding methods and budget justification text, and provide a biosketch and supporting letter for the application.

Caregiver perspective for development and research teams

I am the parent of a child with drug-resistant epilepsy and am available to meet with teams that rarely have direct contact with patients or caregivers. This includes engineering, data science, and modeling groups, device and diagnostic developers, translational researchers, and regulatory or commercial staff.

I share what it was like to search for answers for our daughter, how each decision was actually made, and what has changed for her because of research and engineering like yours. Many teams never meet the children they are working for. Seeing that end goal can provide meaning and drive through the difficult stretches of work.

I am a parent and an engineer, not a clinician. I speak only from our family’s experience and do not provide medical advice.
